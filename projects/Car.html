Peugeot 1914 Grand Prix Model Car

Project Overview — Individual Materials and Manufacturing project focused on taking a historic race car from CAD design through physical manufacturing and then developing a complete manufacturing process and engineering analysis. The project was divided into two parts: hands-on model car manufacturing and individual manufacturing process analysis.

Part 1: Model Car Design & Manufacturing

The first part of the project focused on the hands-on design and manufacturing of a scale model of the Peugeot 1914 Grand Prix car. I designed the model in CAD, developed the individual components and manufacturing approach, and produced the physical model through a combination of 3D printing, molding, machining, and assembly processes.

Design & Manufacturing Objectives

  • Develop a 1:30 scale model of the Peugeot 1914 Grand Prix car based on its original proportions.
  • Create a distinctive wheel design with modified spoke geometry compared with the original group design.
  • Design the model around manufacturing constraints including draft, parting lines, assembly, and material selection.
  • 3D print patterns, molds, and internal components required for manufacturing.
  • Manufacture and assemble the final model using a combination of casting, molding, machining, and additive manufacturing.

Design & Fabrication Process

  • CAD Design: Developed the Peugeot 1914 Grand Prix model in CAD at a 1:30 scale, including the body, wheels, axles, cockpit, and internal components.
  • 3D Printing: Manufactured PLA patterns and internal components using FDM 3D printing.
  • Molding: Created molds and patterns for the cast and molded components while accounting for parting lines, draft, and component geometry.
  • Metal Manufacturing: Manufactured the metal body using the selected casting process and performed post-processing to remove gates, flash, and visible parting lines.
  • Wheel Manufacturing: Produced the model wheels using a molded polymer manufacturing process.
  • Machining: Prepared and finished components using conventional machining and fabrication processes where required.
  • Assembly: Integrated the body, cockpit, wheels, axles, and internal components into the final display model.

Part 2: Manufacturing Process & Engineering Analysis

The second part of the project expanded beyond the physical model and focused on developing and analyzing a complete manufacturing system for the Peugeot 1914 Grand Prix model. I evaluated manufacturing processes, selected materials, designed tooling, and performed engineering calculations to validate the proposed casting and injection molding systems.

Requirements & Design Assumptions

  • Project Car: Peugeot 1914 Grand Prix
  • Scale: 1:30
  • Target Quantity: 1 model
  • Body Material: Aluminum A356
  • Body Process: Sand Casting
  • Wheel Material: Polypropylene (PP)
  • Wheel Process: Injection Molding
  • Axle Material: AISI 1018 Steel
  • Pattern Material: PLA
  • Draft Angle: 2–5° for sand casting surfaces

Manufacturing Process Selection

Sand casting was selected for the aluminum body because the project requires only one model and therefore does not justify the tooling cost of high-pressure die casting. Sand casting also accommodates the complex geometry of the automotive body while allowing the mold and pattern to be produced relatively quickly.

A356 Aluminum Selection

A356 aluminum was selected for the body because of its excellent castability, fluidity, strength-to-weight ratio, and suitability for sand casting. Compared with 1060 aluminum and Zamak 3, A356 provided the best overall combination of casting performance and mechanical properties for the application.

Sand Casting Mold & Gating Analysis

The casting system was designed around a two-part sand mold using a 3D-printed PLA pattern. The parting line was positioned near the widest cross-section of the body to allow the pattern to be removed with a straight pull while minimizing undercuts. The gating system was designed for gravity-fed filling, with the sprue located at the top of the mold and vents positioned at high points of the cavity.

  • Casting Volume: 5.76 in³
  • Surface Area: 33.54 in²
  • Casting Modulus: 0.172 in
  • Riser Modulus: 0.224 in
  • Riser Diameter: 1.12 in
  • Riser Height: 1.12 in
  • Estimated Fill Time: 2.0 s
  • Initial Volumetric Flow Rate: 2.88 in³/s
  • Redesigned Gate Area: 0.073 in²
  • Maximum Gate Velocity: 39.37 in/s

The gating system was revised after the initial velocity calculation exceeded the target ingate velocity. Increasing the gate area reduced the predicted flow velocity to remain within the design constraint and reduce the risk of sand erosion during filling.

Wheel Injection Mold Design

A separate tooling analysis was performed for the model wheels. A single-cavity, two-plate aluminum mold was selected because the project is intended for low-volume prototype production. The wheel was designed around a center-plane parting line with an edge gate positioned on the non-cosmetic inner face near the hub.

  • Mold Configuration: Single cavity
  • Mold Type: Two-plate mold
  • Gate: Edge gate on inner wheel face
  • Ejection: Manual demolding with four optional ejector pins
  • Ejector Pin Diameter: 0.05 in
  • Cooling: Four longitudinal cooling channels
  • Draft Angle: 2°

Injection Mold Machining Plan

  1. Use an edge finder to establish the machining origin.
  2. Spot drill locations for alignment pins.
  3. Drill alignment-pin holes.
  4. Rough the cavity using a 1/4-inch end mill.
  5. Finish the cavity, runners, gates, and ejector features using a 1/8-inch end mill.

Estimated machining time for one mold half was approximately 25 minutes 55 seconds, assuming efficient tool changes and machining operations.

Injection Molding Process Analysis

The mold was subsequently analyzed as an ABS injection molding system. The analysis included shrinkage compensation, shot volume, shot mass, clamping force, cooling time, machine requirements, and overall cycle time.

Design for Moldability

  • Applied a 2° draft angle to vertical surfaces to improve demolding.
  • Placed the parting line through the wheel's widest cross-section.
  • Located the gate on the non-cosmetic inner face to minimize visible gate marks.
  • Positioned ejector pins near reinforced hub and spoke regions to prevent deformation.
  • Designed cooling channels near the mold surface to improve heat transfer and reduce cycle time.
  • Applied shrinkage compensation to the mold cavity dimensions.

Shrinkage Compensation

Shrinkage compensation was applied to account for dimensional changes as the polymer cools. For the ABS analysis, a shrinkage factor of 0.6% was used.

Project Outcome

This project provided an end-to-end manufacturing workflow beginning with CAD design and physical model production and continuing into detailed manufacturing process analysis. The first part emphasized hands-on manufacturing, including 3D printing, molding, casting, machining, finishing, and assembly of the final Peugeot model.

The second part extended the project into engineering analysis by evaluating manufacturing methods, selecting materials, designing casting and injection molding tooling, and calculating process parameters. The final manufacturing strategy combined FDM 3D printing, aluminum sand casting, polymer injection molding, and conventional machining to create a low-volume manufacturing system suited to a one-unit prototype.

Overall, the project strengthened my ability to connect CAD design with real-world manufacturing constraints and use engineering calculations to validate manufacturing decisions.
